# Public informational Instagram examples

## What did we observe in non-AI informational posts?

### Takeaway
On November 1, 2026 I directly inspected three posts on Instagram in a signed-in browser: NASA's seasonal astronomy carousel, National Geographic's wildlife photography post, and Visual Capitalist's languages data graphic. Their subject matter, production resources, audiences and formats vary widely; this is an illustrative sample, not a causal study or proof that these tactics generalize.

### Cited Findings
- [NASA's Halloween astronomy carousel](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfIY71Lku89/) showed 1.4M likes and '2 days ago' at observation; NASA's account showed 104M followers. The opening image uses the Sun's jack-o'-lantern resemblance, the caption ties a seasonal hook to an explanation, invites swiping to other examples, and includes three relevant tags: #NASA #Halloween #Space. A viewer comment reacted specifically to the third image. The count is approximate UI display, not independently measured reach, saves or evidence of hashtag impact.
- [National Geographic's owl photo](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfK8d9qFyfJ/) showed 150.3K likes, 358 comments and '11h' at observation on an account showing 268M followers. Its caption credits the photographer, gives specific context about location, animal behavior and competition, and directs readers to the bio for more; the visible caption had no hashtags. Some public comments discussed the photographed bird's appearance. These are descriptive observations, not evidence that omitting hashtags caused the result.
- [Visual Capitalist's living-languages post](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfKU6R1EVJO/) showed 353 likes and '1 day ago' on an account displaying 414K followers. Its image uses an at-a-glance ranked bar chart and a strong headline; the caption leads with a concrete comparison, gives the data year, names SIL International via Our World in Data, defines the inclusion criterion, and uses five topical tags (#languages #linguistics #culture #papuanewguinea #indonesia). This is one observation, with unknown reach and performance relative to its typical posts.
- [Visual Capitalist's Sunday data digest](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfNVhsfjYIF/) is a carousel whose caption asks three specific questions then invites readers to swipe through the week's data stories; it includes four topic tags (#economy #unemployment #business #companies) and a newsletter CTA. Metrics were not inspected, so do not call it a high-performing post.
- National Geographic's [Everest story carousel](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfK5HEmGlai/) visible caption credits individuals and frames the story around a person, goal, obstacles and a map source (VRICON). Metrics were not inspected.

### Inferences
- For Trywise, try a first slide promising one concrete outcome, then show evidence, a short example and meaningful constraints; choose a visual format that matches the material rather than copying a science photo or dense chart.
- Context, creator/source attribution and relevant topic tags may improve clarity; do not imply the tags or their count produced the displayed reach.
- Seasonal hooks are testable when naturally relevant, but must not trump accurate sourcing or become unrelated hashtag bait.

### Gaps
- No internal follower conversion, impressions, saves, non-follower reach, paid promotion, posting history or distribution data was visible. Public like counts do not establish engagement rate, causal success, or applicability to a zero-follower account.
- Accounts are unusually large (except Visual Capitalist's relatively smaller but still large page); there is no controlled sample of smaller comparable creators and no claim this represents all informational Instagram content.
- Visual Capitalist is a single image, NASA is a carousel, NatGeo is a photo; do not infer a universal best format from these observations. Relative times are as observed on November 1, 2026 and need rechecking later.
