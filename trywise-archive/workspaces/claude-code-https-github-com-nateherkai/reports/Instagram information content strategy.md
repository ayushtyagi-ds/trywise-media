# Build useful posts people can recognize

Trywise AI should test **clear, concrete information posts**, using source-backed examples and strong visuals rather than chasing a universal hashtag formula. Our review on November 1, 2026 looked at specific NASA, National Geographic, and Visual Capitalist posts as well as Meta's own guidance; it is a selective sample, not a study of every successful account. The examples suggest useful formats to test, but their visible likes cannot establish which design or hashtag caused reach. The account had just two posts and zero followers in the latest documented check, so it is too early to declare a winning topic or distribution strategy. The practical next step is to keep daily publishing as a target, publish only well-sourced useful material, compare available results at similar post ages, and change a small number of visible variables at a time.

## Three information accounts use different hooks

NASA's [seasonal astronomy carousel](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfIY71Lku89/) connects an immediately recognizable jack-o'-lantern-like Sun image to scientific explanations and asks readers to swipe; its visible caption used **#NASA, #Halloween, and #Space**. When inspected it showed approximately **1.4 million likes** on an account displaying 104 million followers, with the UI saying two days old. This illustrates a timely visual hook paired with a relevant explanation, not proof that its hashtags produced those likes or that a new page can reproduce that reach.

The [National Geographic owl photo](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfK8d9qFyfJ/) used an arresting image, credited the photographer, and explained the animal's behavior and setting. The visible caption had **no hashtags**; at observation the UI showed approximately 150,300 likes and 358 comments, with 268 million account followers and an 11-hour age. Its usefulness for Trywise is the specific, well-attributed context and strong visual. Account size, distribution, promotion and unavailable saves/reach mean this is not evidence that omitting hashtags caused its performance.

Visual Capitalist's [living-languages post](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfKU6R1EVJO/) led with an at-a-glance ranked comparison, supplied its data year and source, and named inclusion criteria. Its five topical tags accompanied a concrete chart; the observed display showed 353 likes and a one-day age, with 414,000 account followers. The [weekly data digest](https://www.instagram.com/p/DfNVhsfjYIF/) framed its carousel as questions readers could answer by swiping. We did not inspect digest metrics, so it is an example of structure, not a proven high-performing post. For Trywise, test a title like **“Turn messy meeting notes into a clear action list”**, followed by an original fictional input, a copyable prompt, an example output labeled illustrative, and a privacy/accuracy check. Do not reuse third-party images or present a mockup as an actual test.

## Relevant hashtags are a test, not a growth guarantee

Use **zero to five hashtags that describe the specific post**, rather than a fixed block on every item. For example, a post about drafting a meeting summary might test `#AIProductivity` and `#MeetingNotes`; a ChatGPT-specific demonstration could test `#ChatGPTTips` if the content actually covers that product. These are illustrative hypotheses, not tags validated as high-performing for this account. Natural language in the cover and caption should describe the topic clearly even without tags.

In December 2025, reporting quoted Instagram's @creators account saying that a gradual change would limit posts and Reels to five hashtags. The original [Threads announcement](https://www.threads.com/@creators/post/DSalXGPCWM4) was rate-limited during this research; [The Verge's report](https://www.theverge.com/news/847739/instagram-hashtag-spam-limit) describes the announcement. **We have not verified whether or how this account's Buffer/API path enforces that limit.** Keep the recommendation conservative, check behavior before relying on it, and do not imply that five is an algorithmically optimal number.

## Design for an audience and measure what happens

Meta describes ranking systems that combine several predictions and signals, including likely shares, with different systems for different surfaces ([Meta explanation](https://about.fb.com/news/2023/06/how-ai-ranks-content-on-facebook-and-instagram/)). Meta's [creator best-practices hub](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/10/best-practices-education-hub-creators-instagram/) offers guidance and personalized insights, but we have not verified its availability for this account. No source establishes a universal best posting hour, fixed caption length, mandatory three-second cut, or guaranteed hashtag recipe.

For a two-week pilot, rotate a **practical task**, a **source-checked news explainer**, and an **original developer example**. Write the cover as a clear benefit or relatable problem, then make each subsequent slide advance the explanation: what to do, an example, what to check, and a source or limitation. Produce legible original visuals suited to the idea, rather than adding motion or decorative graphics for their own sake. Add a natural question or invitation only when it fits the content; do not plant mistakes or promise a result that has not been tested.

Record each post's topic, format, first-slide wording, hashtags and posting time. At roughly **24 and 72 hours**, inspect whichever metrics Instagram makes available: unique reach, views, saves, shares, profile visits and follows. Views and reach are not identical, and a missing or delayed metric is not zero. Compare posts of similar age and type; change one prominent variable where practical, but avoid calling a small uncontrolled comparison a statistically reliable result. Daily posts are a goal, not a reason to publish unverified or low-value content. One initial automated post reused an existing draft, which is evidence of that route working—not proof that fully automated production of new posts is reliable.

For Reels, consider testing a short original screen recording that demonstrates a real task. If available, Meta describes [Trial Reels](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/12/trial-reels-try-content-non-followers-first-see-what-perfoms-best/) that initially reach non-followers and expose early metrics around 24 hours; **eligibility and support through Buffer have not been verified**. A screen recording requires a real demonstration and the right to use any third-party assets. There is no evidence here that following or liking other creators mechanically boosts our own distribution, though selective following can help us research their work.

## What this changes for Trywise AI

The research supports improving the next posts' **specificity and useful examples**, with hashtags chosen case by case, rather than copying high-follower accounts' visual assets or asserting their like counts reveal a secret. Keep the owner’s CAD $50 budget, existing approval for automatic Instagram publishing, source checks, no-duplicate controls, and reporting of failed runs. After a small set of comparable posts and actual observations, choose the next experiment from the available data; audience growth remains uncertain.
